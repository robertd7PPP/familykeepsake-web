The pile

You can't keep it all. And you can't bin it.

Every week they bring home more: a painting still wet at the corners, a swimming certificate, a Lego spaceship you're not allowed to touch. Some of it makes the fridge door. Most of it goes into a bag in a cupboard, waiting for a wet Sunday that never comes.

Photographing it should be the answer — but those photos sink into a camera roll full of screenshots and receipts, unnamed, undated, unfindable. Keeping the memory shouldn't mean typing out who-what-when three hundred times.

FamilyKeepsake makes the photo do the work.

  • ✓ Photograph it once — it's named, dated and filed
  • ✓ The painting can leave; the keepsake stays
  • ✓ Every word on it is findable, years later
  • ✓ The whole collection plays back in Rewind

Privacy

Your kids' things never leave your family.

Most keepsake apps upload your children's work to their cloud and charge you monthly to keep it there. FamilyKeepsake is built the other way around: the AI reads on your device, the files live in your iCloud, and there is no "our cloud" at all.

Nothing uploaded

The reading happens on your iPhone or iPad. It works in flight mode.

No account

No sign-up, no email, no password. The app never asks who you are.

Your iCloud, not ours

Files and sync ride on the Apple Account your family already owns.

No analytics, no ads

The app phones no one. Nothing is tracked, measured or sold.

No subscription

There are no servers, so there's nothing for a monthly fee to fund.

About things, not faces

It catalogues what your kids make — the artefacts, not the children.

Why we can promise this

FamilyKeepsake has no servers. The AI is Apple silicon in your hand; the storage is the iCloud your family already pays for. There is nowhere for your kids' things to go — and that isn't a policy that could change one day. It's the architecture.

FAQ

Questions parents ask

Where are my kids' keepsakes stored?

In your family's own iCloud Drive, as ordinary photos and PDFs with readable names, organised into folders by category. FamilyKeepsake has no servers — we couldn't hold your photos if we wanted to.

Does the AI upload my photos anywhere?

No. The reading happens on your iPhone or iPad using Apple Intelligence. It works in flight mode. Nothing is sent to us or anyone else.

What happens if I stop using FamilyKeepsake?

Everything stays. Your keepsakes are ordinary files in your own iCloud Drive — named, foldered, and readable by any app. The archive outlives the app, by design.

Is it a subscription?

No — and it never will be. Photographing or scanning one thing at a time is free forever. A single NZ$29.99 purchase adds multi-photo keepsakes and the bulk import lanes. There is no subscription because there are no servers to pay for.

What's free, and what does the unlock buy?

Free forever: capture with the camera or document scanner, import a photo from your library, full AI reading, unlimited keepsakes, search, Rewind and voice notes. The one-time unlock adds several photos of one keepsake, and opens the bulk lanes: import whole albums from Photos, import school-report PDFs, and share into the app from anywhere.

Which devices does it need?

FamilyKeepsake is built on Apple Intelligence, so it needs iOS 27 on an iPhone 15 Pro or newer, or an iPad with an M-series chip. That's what makes the on-device reading — and the privacy promise — possible.

How does it know which of my kids made something?

Kids sign their work. FamilyKeepsake reads the name — even a wobbly one in a corner — and matches it against your family. A joint creation can belong to more than one child.

What about school reports?

Import them as PDFs. The real multi-page PDF is archived in your iCloud Drive, every page is read, and school, year and term are filled in from your family's school timeline.

What if it reads something wrong?

You always see the card before it saves, and every field can be fixed with a tap. Filenames are never built from AI guesses — only from confirmed facts: child, type and date.
